## Post body

Five days ago I wrote that the price of AI coding agents will go dramatically up.

The argument: a $200/month tool that replaces weeks of engineering labor is absurdly underpriced. Current pricing is the land grab — like ride-sharing subsidies before the prices went up.

Today The Verge published "You're about to feel the AI money squeeze." The numbers are staggering: to hit even a bare-minimum 7% return on $6.3 trillion in AI data center investment, providers need roughly $2 trillion per year in AI revenue by 2029. Token consumption would need to grow 50,000-100,000x. Every company is currently losing money on the newer, token-hungry models.

One quote stood out: "The free era was really a land grab. That is just not a business model. You cannot do that for too long."

I used the same words. Same analogy. Five days earlier.

The part nobody is talking about yet: for coding specifically, there is no cheap alternative. As one CEO in the article puts it: "If you are doing coding of any kind, the open-source models simply don't come close." That means coding agents have pricing power that general chatbots do not.
