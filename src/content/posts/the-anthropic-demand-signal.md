A source inside a credit card issuer told me something I have not seen reported anywhere: the spike in transactions to buy Anthropic services this year was of such epic proportions that it sent their fraud detection systems nuts. Thousands of businesses, more or less simultaneously, starting to pay the same vendor looks — to a fraud model — like something is wrong.

Nothing was wrong. It was demand.

The public data backs the anecdote. Ramp, which sees the card spend of 70,000+ US businesses, reports that in July 43.5% of them paid for Anthropic — overtaking OpenAI at 39.7%. IDC measured $89.7 billion of AI infrastructure spend in Q1 alone, up 33% year over year, and raised its full-year forecast to $497 billion. Stripe found the top 100 AI companies on its platform reached $1 million in annualized revenue in a median 11.5 months — four months faster than the best SaaS cohort ever did. This is processed payment volume, not projections.

Two caveats, because the same data cuts the other way.

First, growth in adoption breadth is slowing: Anthropic's share of paying businesses grew 1.1 points month over month, OpenAI's just 0.23. Ramp titled its own report "Cracks in the AI thesis." The boring explanation is saturation — when 43.5% of a tech-leaning panel already pays, there is less panel left to convert.

Second, the spend is extremely concentrated. The top 1% of firms spend a median $7,400 per employee on AI. The median firm spends $11.95. That is not a typo — twelve dollars. A small group of companies is going all in, and most of the economy is buying a ChatGPT seat or two.

So both readings are true at once. The demand is real, paid, and fast enough to trip fraud models. And the adoption is narrow — most businesses have barely started. Whether you find that bullish or bearish depends on whether you think the median firm eventually looks like the top 1%, or the top 1% is early proof of a bubble.

I know which side I am on. That is the next post.
