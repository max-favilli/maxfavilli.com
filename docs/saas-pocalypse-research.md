## What happened

In early 2026, ~$300B-$1T in SaaS market cap evaporated in days after AI agent products (particularly Claude Code/Cowork launches) demonstrated they could replace entire workflow categories. The IGV Software Index dropped ~30% from September 2025 peaks. Forward earnings multiples for software companies collapsed from ~39x to ~21x. Short sellers netted over $20B betting against legacy SaaS.

## Steve Eisman's angle

Eisman identified two distinct but connected threats:

1. **The "SaaS apocalypse"** — clients rushing to replace SaaS workflows with AI agents, hollowing out seat-based revenue. He flagged IBM's pre-announced miss as early evidence.

2. **AI's own Achilles' heel** — the AI companies themselves lack moats. OpenAI and Anthropic account for ~70% of AI revenue at Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Oracle, and 25-35% of their cloud revenue. But Chinese competitors (Moonshot's Kimi K3 at $3/M tokens vs Anthropic's $10) signal a price war. Eisman: "If I was the head of Anthropic or OpenAI, I'd be petrified." He sold his Alphabet stake and reduced AI exposure.

## The bear case (SaaS is structurally threatened)

- **Build vs. buy has flipped** — AI coding tools make custom software fast and cheap enough to compete with SaaS subscriptions. Klarna ditched Salesforce CRM for a homegrown AI system.
- **Seat-based pricing breaks** — when one AI agent does the work of five employees, per-seat revenue collapses.
- **Agents become the interface** — users stop interacting with SaaS UIs and work through AI agents instead, making SaaS products "invisible infrastructure."
- **Most vulnerable categories**: customer support, CRM, simple analytics, HR tools, legal services — anything where the value is workflow convenience, not proprietary data.
- Goldman Sachs: by 2030, over 60% of software economics could flow through agentic systems rather than legacy seats.

## The bull case (SaaS adapts and survives)

- **Enterprise complexity persists** — compliance, security, governance, audit infrastructure took years to build and can't be "vibe-coded" in a weekend.
- **Data moats get stronger** — Intuit's 100M customer tax/credit records, Spotify's music dataset — AI makes proprietary data more valuable, not less.
- **MCP integration** — Intuit, HubSpot, Salesforce, DocuSign opened Model Context Protocol endpoints, becoming the capability layer agents work through.
- **Building != maintaining** — "Creating an application is trivial compared to maintaining it" (Jeremiah Stone, SnapLogic). Churn, security, scaling, ops don't disappear.
- **Global SaaS spending still growing** — projected from $318B (2025) to $576B (2029). Vertical SaaS market expanding from $133B to $194B.
- **Market recovering** — by June 2026, software stocks up 13%. Orlando Bravo (Thoma Bravo): "AI is an enormous tailwind for software companies."
- Forrester/Forbes: "SaaSpocalypse is dead" may be premature, but the worst market panic is behind.

## The emerging consensus

Not SaaS death, but **bifurcation**:
- **Winners**: platform companies with data moats, compliance infrastructure, and agent-native APIs (Salesforce Agentforce, Intuit GenOS)
- **Losers**: narrow point-solution SaaS with seat-based pricing, low switching costs, and easily replicable workflows
- **Pricing shift**: from per-seat to consumption-based (tokens) and outcome-based (pay for results). Sierra hit $100M ARR in under 2 years on outcome pricing.
- **The real insight**: "Software increasingly looks like a container. Intelligence becomes the product."

## Deep-research findings (verified, Aug 2026)

106-agent research run; 24 sources fetched, 118 claims extracted, 25 adversarially verified (23 confirmed, 2 refuted).

### Vendor knowledge moat — validated but contested (Max's angle #2)

- **Vista Equity Partners (Jan 2026)** — the analyst-grade version of the SAP argument: incumbents control decades of proprietary structured data that "defines what constitutes a valid transaction, a compliant outcome or an exception." And: "While AI lowers the cost of writing code, it does not lower the cost of defining, testing and maintaining workflows... real-life edge cases that can take years to document." Caveat: Vista is a PE firm long enterprise software. [Source](https://www.vistaequitypartners.com/insights/who-wins-as-enterprises-scale-ai/)
- **SAP CEO Christian Klein, Sapphire 2026 (May)** — first-party articulation: applications matter because they "encode decades of process logic that no foundation model can learn from public data alone"; SAP merging LLMs with "7.3 million data fields and built-in governance"; launched Autonomous Suite (50+ Joule Assistants, 200+ AI agents, e.g. Autonomous Close Assistant). Incumbent positioning itself as disruptor. [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/victordey/2026/05/12/the-end-of-the-erp-era-sap-wants-ai-agents-to-run-your-autonomous-enterprise/) / [SAP press release](https://news.sap.com/2026/05/sap-sapphire-sap-unveils-autonomous-enterprise/)
- **Counter: Bessemer Venture Partners** — argues the two deepest systems-of-record moats (implementation/customization cost, housing customers' critical data) are exactly what AI erodes; legacy vendors "vulnerable for the first time in decades." Caveat: Bessemer funds AI-native challengers; published May 2025. Do NOT cite their "90% integration time reduction" figure — refuted in verification. [Source](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/roadmap-ai-systems-of-action)
- Net: a live incumbent-vs-challenger debate where each side's financial incentives align with its conclusion. Open question nobody engages: can AI agents reconstruct SAP/Veeva-class configuration logic from outside the walled garden?

### Demand signals — corroborates the Anthropic anecdote (Max's angle #4)

- **Ramp AI Index (Aug 2026, card-transaction data from 70,000+ businesses)**: 43.5% of US businesses paid for Anthropic in July 2026, overtaking OpenAI (39.7%). BUT growth decelerating (Anthropic +1.1pts MoM, OpenAI +0.23pts) and spend extremely concentrated: top 1% of firms spend median $7,400/employee vs $11.95 at the median firm. Ramp's own title: "Cracks in the AI thesis." Panel skews SMB/tech-forward. [Source](https://ramp.com/data/ai-index-august-2026)
- **IDC**: $89.7B AI infrastructure spend in Q1 2026 (+33% YoY); full-year 2026 forecast raised to $497B (+56% vs 2025); $1.08T projected by 2029. "Sustained, multi-year capital commitment cycle." [Source](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/ai-infrastructure-spending-holds-near-90-billion-in-q1-2026-as-arm-overtakes-x86-in-accelerated-servers-2026-forecast-raised-to-497-billion/)
- **Stripe**: top 100 AI companies hit $1M annualized revenue in median 11.5 months — ~4 months faster than the fastest SaaS cohort. Realized processed revenue, not projections (data vintage ~2024-25). [Source](https://stripe.com/guides/indexing-the-ai-economy)

### Experienced-engineer multiplier — weakest evidence, handle honestly (Max's angle #3)

- **For**: Fastly survey (791 US devs, Jul 2025): 32% of seniors (10+ yrs) ship majority-AI code vs 13% of juniors — ~2.5x. Attributed to seniors' ability to catch AI's mistakes. Self-reported adoption share, not a measured productivity multiplier. [Source](https://www.fastly.com/blog/senior-developers-ship-more-ai-code)
- **Against**: METR RCT (2025): AI tools made experienced open-source devs ~20% SLOWER (+19%, CI +2% to +39%) while they believed they were faster. (METR's late-2025 "reversal to speedup" was refuted — METR itself calls it very weak evidence; don't cite.) [Source](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)
- **Against**: DX telemetry (~400 orgs, Nov 2024-Feb 2026): AI usage +65%, PR throughput only +7.76%. "10%, not 10x." [Source](https://getdx.com/blog/ai-productivity-gains-are-10-percent-not-10x/)
- Prior field studies found the OPPOSITE direction: juniors gain more. DORA 2026: 35-40% gains on simple greenfield work, ~10% or less on complex legacy code.
- **Post framing**: my 30 years' lived experience diverges from measured averages — say so explicitly and argue why the studies miss what practitioners see (e.g., they measure PR throughput on existing codebases, not the weeks-to-half-a-day compression on greenfield builds).

### SI/consulting second-order effects — capturing value, not contracting

- Accenture: $2.7B AI revenue FY25 (+200% YoY), $5.9B AI bookings (+100%); Q1 FY26 AI revenue +120%. Infosys: $4.8B large deals in fiscal Q3 (+57% QoQ), AI-led deals margin-accretive per management.
- Industry structurally repricing from time-and-materials to outcome/consumption-based "services as software": Cognizant 47% of Q3 2025 sales outcome-based; ExlService 36% targeting mid-40%; Grid Dynamics, Globant, Endava same shift. The pyramid/headcount model is being replaced, not the firms. [William Blair equity research, Jan 2026](https://www.williamblair.com/-/media/downloads/eqr/2026/williamblair_entering-2026-ai-pricing-takes-hold-stabilizing-demand-strategic-ma.pdf)
- No verified data on Deloitte or Wipro specifically.

### "Priced in / overdone" contrarians — open lane

- No credible mid-2026 source arguing the SaaS-pocalypse is already priced into equities survived verification. Closest: Jim Cramer defending ServiceNow/Salesforce against "extinction fears" (Aug 19, 2026); ServiceNow's Q2 beat. This angle is unclaimed territory for the post.

## Author's angles (Max's notes)

1. **Data is more king than before** — agrees with the bull case on data moats. Proprietary data becomes the real defensible asset in an AI world.

2. **Walled-garden knowledge becomes a moat** — SAP's protected knowledge base is an example. Only SAP's own AI has full access to all SAP's proprietary material (documentation, implementation guides, best practices, configuration logic). Competitor AI vendors can't access this, making SAP potentially more protected than before, not less. This extends to any vendor sitting on deep, proprietary domain knowledge behind a walled garden.

3. **The build speed is real** — from 30 years of software experience: what took weeks now takes half a day with AI. And when experienced software engineers guide the AI, the results are stellar. This is not hype — it's happening now, and it validates the bear case for simple SaaS.

4. **Anthropic demand signal** — source inside a credit card issuer reports the spike in transactions to buy Anthropic services is of epic proportions, enough to trigger fraud detection systems. This is a real-world signal of how fast AI adoption is accelerating at the infrastructure level.

## Refined thesis (after deep research, Aug 2026)

1. **Jevons paradox rules the SAP question — Klein and Bessemer are both right, on different layers.** The knowledge moat (decades of process logic behind the walled garden) holds; the friction moat (implementation cost) erodes. SAP doesn't sell implementation hours — the SI pyramid does. Implementation historically cost 2-5x the license fee and was the biggest friction in every SAP sale. AI collapsing it lowers SAP's effective TCO, opening the mid-market that could never afford an S/4 migration: Jevons working FOR SAP (cheaper implementation → more viable projects → more license/cloud revenue). The compression lands on SI headcount instead. Double edge to acknowledge: implementation cost was also a switching-cost moat — if implementing gets 10x cheaper, so does migrating off SAP or onto a challenger. Jevons expands the market while thinning the walls around every player. Nobody has written this framing.

2. **On the "deceleration" caveat** — it's narrow: Ramp measures adoption-breadth growth (share of businesses paying) slowing in a tech-skewed SMB panel where 43.5% already pay. Panel saturation is the boring explanation. Not revenue decline, not usage decline. xAI and open-source platforms still accelerating in the same report.

3. **The productivity studies measure the wrong thing.** Fastly = self-reported share of AI-generated shipped code (a survey). DX = merged-PR throughput. METR = time on ~2-hour tasks by experienced OSS maintainers on their own mature legacy repos — nearly the inverse of greenfield senior-guided agentic delivery, which is where the weeks-to-half-a-day compression happens (JW experience) and which nobody has measured. PRs, LoC, and tickets closed are effort proxies, not value: different developers produce different code, with or without AI. The market already agrees: the SI industry is abandoning effort-based billing for outcome-based contracts (Cognizant 47%). The market that prices software work has concluded effort metrics no longer track value — while researchers still count PRs.

4. **The SI data confirms both Jevons and point 3** — Accenture's AI bookings doubling while delivery gets cheaper is demand expanding faster than effort compresses; the pricing-model shift is the revenue-side proof that effort no longer tracks value. Caveat: "AI revenue" is partly self-defined/rebadged and only ~4% of Accenture's total.

5. **The skepticism gap is the story's hook.** Most managers/directors outside IT don't believe the change; CFOs are skeptical. The data quantifies it: top 1% of firms spend median $7,400/employee on AI vs $11.95 at the median firm (Ramp); 80% of software buyers cite AI commoditization as #1 risk vs 25% of SaaS CEOs. Honest framing required: "real change most people don't believe yet" describes both early regime changes AND bubbles — distinguishable only by hard signals (real payment volume, real delivery-time compression witnessed firsthand, real repricing of the services industry).
